---
title: "Cultivating Curiosity in an Ordinary Day"
date: "2026-08-18"
slug: "cultivating-curiosity-in-an-ordinary-day"
---

I recently found a short line in my notes: “Curiosity cultivator—where to eat,
sit, walk, read.” I do not remember exactly when I wrote it, but I think I was
trying to capture a simple idea: perhaps curiosity is something I can practise
through the small choices I make each day.

It is easy for those choices to become automatic. I take the route I already
know, eat somewhere familiar, sit in the same kind of place, and read about
subjects that already interest me. There is nothing wrong with that. Routine is
useful, and familiarity often makes life easier. But if I always make the most
predictable choice, I also reduce the opportunity to encounter something new.

One of my favourite examples happened during a random walk with my girlfriend
by the Water of Leith. We came across an amazing band performing in a garden.
It was completely unexpected and became a highlight for both of us. The walk
did not need a grand plan to become memorable. We simply went somewhere and
left enough room for an ordinary day to surprise us.

That is the kind of experience I would like to create more opportunities for.
Not every different route will lead to live music in a garden, of course. Most
will probably just be different routes. But even then, I might notice a building
I have never properly looked at, an unusual sign, a new place to eat, or the
people moving through a part of the city I usually pass without attention.

I already enjoy people watching. Sitting somewhere and observing the different
ways people behave, speak, dress, and interact can make a familiar place feel
interesting again. I also like trying new restaurants, where choosing a place I
have not visited gives me a small break from the predictable. I would like to
extend that same attitude into other parts of my life: trying unfamiliar
recipes, picking up a book outside my usual interests, sitting somewhere else,
or taking a different route to a destination I know well.

This is not about constantly chasing novelty. Turning every lunch or journey
into an “experience” would become tiring, and routine has its own value. I am
more interested in occasionally interrupting the default. When there is no good
reason to choose the usual option, I can choose another one and pay attention
to what changes.

The paying-attention part may matter as much as the choice itself. A different
route is not very useful if I spend the whole walk looking at my phone. I want
to look around more deliberately: at buildings, signs, people, and the small
details that distinguish one street from another. Curiosity is partly about
going somewhere unfamiliar, but it is also about noticing what is already in
front of me.

So this is a small personal experiment. I want to introduce minor variations
into otherwise ordinary days and see what they produce. Take another street.
Order something unfamiliar. Cook a recipe I have never tried. Read a book I
would not normally pick up. Sit somewhere else and observe.

Most of these choices may lead to nothing remarkable. Occasionally, though,
one might lead to a conversation, an idea, a place worth returning to, or an
amazing band playing in a garden. That seems reason enough to leave a little
more space for curiosity.
